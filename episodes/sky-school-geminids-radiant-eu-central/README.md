### Post copy

**Why meteors seem to come from one point in the sky 🌠**

The Geminids look like they come from a single point in Gemini, the radiant. They do not: the meteors are debris from asteroid 3200 Phaethon, and they all travel on parallel paths. We meet the stream head-on, so those paths seem to fan out from a point ahead of us. At the peak the radiant is 73° up in the south, where the ideal rate is up to 150 meteors an hour.

Image: radar imagery of Phaethon, NASA / JPL / Arecibo Observatory. Positions computed for central Europe (50°N, 15°E) with astronomy-engine. Narration uses a synthetic voice.
