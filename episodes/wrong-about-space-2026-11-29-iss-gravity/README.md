### Post copy

**Astronauts don't float because there's no gravity 🧑‍🚀**

At the International Space Station's altitude (200–250 miles, about 320–400 km), Earth's gravity is still about 90% as strong as on the ground. Astronauts float because they're in free fall: the station, the crew and everything aboard are constantly falling toward Earth while racing sideways at 17,500 mph (about 28,000 km/h), so they keep falling around it instead.

Sources:
NASA, What Is Microgravity? (Grades 5-8): https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/for-kids-and-students/what-is-microgravity-grades-5-8/
NASA Glenn, What is Microgravity?: https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/glenn/what-is-microgravity/

Images: NASA / JSC (ISS from a departing Soyuz, 2018; Jessica Meir with a floating water sphere, 2020). Narration uses a synthetic voice.
