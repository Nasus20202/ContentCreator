# Series: Sky Tonight

**id:** `sky-tonight` · **format:** vertical short, 15–25 s · **cadence:** ad hoc, on the day of or the day before a high-value event · **research:** `cc research sky` on the event window

## Promise to the viewer

> "Tonight: the one thing worth going outside for — when to look, where, and for how long. Twenty seconds."

## When this series is made

Only when one event is strong enough to stand alone: a meteor peak with a dark Moon, an opposition, an eclipse, or a conjunction under 1°. It is a reminder, not a second weekly episode:

- the weekly episode explains the week; this one makes tonight urgent and concrete;
- publish on the day of (or the day before) the best night, after the weekly episode has already gone out;
- if the event is uncertain (clouds, weather, an active Sun), do not publish it.

## Episode structure

| Beat       | Seconds  | Template            | Purpose                                                 |
| ---------- | -------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hook       | 0–4      | `skyView` + `title` | Name the event and the night                            |
| When/where | 4–16     | `skyView`           | Time window, direction, height, naked eye vs binoculars |
| Outro      | last 4–5 | `outro`             | Location footnote + one-line follow ask                 |

## Writing rules (in addition to docs/quality-bar.md)

- Say "tonight" and the local time window, never a UTC time.
- One number the viewer can use (when to look, or how many per hour). Not three.
- No "don't miss it" urgency unless the event is genuinely one-night-only.
- If the Moon interferes, say so and give the workaround (later/earlier hours).

## Visual rules

- One `skyView`, one camera move, at most two labels.
- The `title` overlay carries the date, so the viewer can screenshot it.
- No chips unless there is a single time the viewer must remember.

## Hashtags baseline

`#astronomy #stargazing #tonight #nightsky` + 1–2 event-specific tags.
